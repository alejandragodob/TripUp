# The brief — TripUp design challenge (summary of the original)

## Context
Friend trips are great; organizing them is not. Deciding where to go, stay, eat and what to do — plus managing expenses and keeping everyone in the loop — gets frustrating fast. TripUp is a new mobile app: an all-in-one platform for organizing group travel (weekend getaway to festival abroad). Friends plan the trip, shape it as it unfolds, collaboratively build itineraries, vote on decisions, track and settle expenses. The beta has traction; the team wants a full redesign.

## User insights
**Profiles**
- The Organizer — most proactive; sets up the trip, invites others, keeps things moving.
- The Participants — want to be involved without being overwhelmed; suggest ideas, vote, stay informed.

**Behavior patterns**
- Spontaneous decision-making — most group choices (e.g. where to eat) happen on the fly, mid-trip. Must support quick, real-time interactions.
- Shared ownership — one person often leads, but everyone wants input, especially on food, activities, expenses.
- Low tolerance for friction — more than a couple of taps and users switch to WhatsApp. Simplicity is essential.

**Preferences**
- Polls over chats — quick tools like polls beat drawn-out chats, if fast and intuitive.
- Simple expense tracking — easy ways to manage and settle group expenses, incl. in-app payments via bank transfers or other digital methods.
- Real-time feedback — when decisions are made, users expect instant updates reflected in the itinerary and notifications.

## User scenario
Last evening of a group trip to Lisbon. After sightseeing the group is back at the house getting ready to go out. Ari (a participant) opens TripUp and lands on the home screen listing all her trips. She taps the current trip to open the group view and adds Ren, a new friend joining for the final dinner.

Ari creates a poll to choose where to eat: three nearby restaurants with different vibes. The app notifies everyone. As votes come in, the leading option updates in real time. When the poll closes, the winning spot is automatically added to the itinerary.

After dinner Ari logs the expenses. She excludes Ren and Nic from the wine cost; the app updates each person's total trip balance. TripUp consolidates the debts to minimize transfers and the friends settle their shares directly in the app. A confirmation lets the group know everything is squared up.

## Deliverables (three escalating layers of fidelity)
1. **Wireflow — the thinking.** Captures the scenario; key decisions, state changes, interaction patterns. Lean (≈10 screens or fewer), real content, in a dedicated Figma section.
2. **High-fidelity screens — the craft.** Two key screens at full visual fidelity; shows design direction and a coherent visual language, independent of AI output.
3. **Interactive prototype — the result.** Using an AI-assisted code tool, turn the wireflow into a working, mobile-optimized prototype that walks through the full journey at demo fidelity. Hi-fi screens must appear in it. Mock data is fine. **Primary deliverable** — the artifact a PM could demo. Figma click-through does not count; must be real code producing a functional web or mobile app.

Design for modern smartphones (iPhone 14+ or Android equivalent); pick one device and keep its screen dimensions. Chosen: **iPhone 15, 393 × 852.**

## What they look for
- Recognize when competitors (e.g. Splitwise) made good UX choices, when to improve them, when to take a different approach; bring features together cohesively.
- Evaluation questions: Is the scenario completely satisfied? Is it usable without guidance? Are screens polished and consistent? Does the prototype faithfully represent the design? Could a PM interact with it and understand the product? Did you add value beyond the brief?
